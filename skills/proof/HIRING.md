# Using proofs in hiring

This file is for a recipient, not a candidate. It applies to any organization that uses this skill.

- **A proof is evidence, not a score.** The outcome word says whether the claims survived re-derivation against the public repository. It does not say whether to hire. Reproduced means the mechanics check out. Contradicted is a real negative signal. Inconclusive means re-run it or ask; do not assume the worst. Not reproducible is the common case for private work, and it carries no penalty by itself.
- **A person makes the decision.** This skill produces evidence for a person to weigh, never a hiring verdict. Some jurisdictions regulate AI that evaluates candidates. The EU AI Act classifies it as high-risk, and GDPR restricts decisions made solely by automated means. Check with counsel before you rely on it.
- **Ask only for work the candidate has the right to share.** Employment contracts, NDAs, and client agreements often forbid it. Never ask someone to break a confidentiality obligation to prove themselves.
- **Offer another route for private work.** Most professional engineering happens in private repositories. Offer a take-home, a pairing session, or a portfolio review, and never score a candidate down for a proof that comes back Not reproducible.
- **Never rank candidates on commit counts or contributor rank.** Both reward volume, not skill, and candidates learn to game them once they know you count. Read the claims and their evidence instead.
- **Never accept a `verified.html` or `verification.json` from a candidate.** Verification means something only when your own copy of the skill produced it, against your own clone. A file the candidate generated proves only that they can run a script.
- **Use the interview questions.** Each audited claim carries one question that the person who did the work can answer in about two minutes. Bring them into the interview, whatever the outcome.
